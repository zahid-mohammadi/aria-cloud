# Theory of Change — AriaCloud (one page)

## Problem

Afghan businesses, students, and — most acutely — women face near-insurmountable
barriers to going online: payments require a foreign card, tools are English-only
and bandwidth-heavy, and there is no local-language AI. The result is exclusion
from the digital economy exactly where it matters most.

## The causal claim

**If** we remove the two largest barriers — a foreign payment card and English-only,
high-bandwidth tools — **then** Afghan businesses, students, and women can come online
**because** they can pay in Afghani through HesabPay and operate in Dari/Pashto on a
2G phone, **which leads to** digitized businesses, local learning, and women's
remote economic participation, **contributing to** a self-sustaining Afghan digital
economy rather than continued dependence on foreign platforms.

## Assumptions (state and test them)

1. HesabPay reaches the target users and processes payments reliably.
2. Dari/Pashto-first is genuinely preferred over English-first tools.
3. A 2G/WhatsApp-native experience is meaningfully more accessible than web apps.
4. Business tiers generate enough cross-subsidy to sustain Free/Student tiers.
5. The operating environment remains permissive enough to operate (compliance).

## The bridge ask

Fixed infrastructure + a small team until **~100 paying users** make the platform
self-sustaining — funded for the gap, not forever.
