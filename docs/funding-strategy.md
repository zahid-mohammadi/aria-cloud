# Funding Strategy — AriaCloud

**Status:** living document. The platform is built and verified; this covers how
to fund the fixed-cost bridge and scale, honestly accounting for the
Afghanistan-compliance reality.

---

## 1. What we are selling to funders

Donors fund outcomes, not infrastructure. The fundable story is:

> **Affordable digital and economic access for Afghanistan — in Dari and Pashto,
> paid locally, working on a 2G phone.**

The technology is the *means*, never the ask.

---

## 2. The four strongest angles

1. **Language access / digital inclusion** — no one serves Dari and *especially*
   Pashto well. This is a rare, specific, defensible gap.
2. **Women's economic participation** — post-2021, remote/digital work is one of
   the few legal pathways for Afghan women. A low-bandwidth platform usable from
   home is exactly what gender-focused funders prioritize.
3. **Education & youth** — students, exam prep, and skills in local languages.
4. **SME / economic resilience** — digitizing Afghan businesses, local HesabPay
   payments, cross-subsidy keeping it affordable.

**Lead with 1 + 2** — most differentiated and most fundable.

## 3. Funder landscape (priority order)

| Category | Funders | Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| In-country foundations | Aga Khan Foundation (AKDN), Asia Foundation, Mercy Corps | Operate in Afghanistan; fund digital/education/economic inclusion |
| UN / multilateral | UNDP, UNICEF (Innovation Fund), UNHCR, WFP, ITU | Active in Afghanistan; run innovation funds + pilots |
| European bilateral | GIZ, FCDO, Sida, EU, NORAD | More room than US funders post-2021 |
| Tech / AI-for-good | Google.org, Microsoft AI for Good, Cisco Foundation, AWS Imagine | AI-in-local-languages story fits |
| Fintech / digital finance | Gates Foundation, Mastercard Foundation, Omidyar Network, GSMA | HesabPay/local payments + identity + youth |
| Diaspora philanthropy | Afghan diaspora foundations + HNIs (US/EU/Gulf) | Large, motivated, remittance-connected |

**Also:** register the platform as a **Digital Public Good (DPGA)** — a low-cost
legitimacy signal that unlocks funding and partnerships.

---

## 4. The honest constraint: sanctions

Post-2021 Afghanistan restrictions will filter out some funders — especially
US-government-linked (USAID/DIV) and some US foundations. This is real.

Two things work in our favor:

1. **Non-US funders** (EU, GIZ, FCDO, Scandinavian, Gulf, Asian, multilaterals)
   have more room.
2. **Our compliance posture is a selling point** — HesabPay-only payments, OFAC
   screening, KYC, and a documented compliance model. Risk-averse donors fund
   teams that take compliance seriously. Lead with it, don't hide it.

---

## 5. What to prepare

1. **Theory of change** — see `theory-of-change.md` (one page).
2. **Impact metrics** — define now so the pilot generates them: students reached,
   businesses digitized, women users, AFN transacted, provinces reached.
3. **Pitch deck** — social-impact narrative + the cost-recovery pricing model
   (`pricing-plan.md`) as the sustainability story.
4. **A pilot with real numbers** — the working product is the evidence; get
   50–100 users and measure.
5. **Compliance one-pager** — polish `compliance-gaps.md` into a donor-ready memo.
6. **A budget tied to the fixed-cost bridge** — the honest ask.

---

## 6. The ask (one sentence)

> **Bridge funding of ~$50–150K to cover fixed infrastructure and a small team
> until ~100 paying users make it self-sustaining — in exchange for a pilot that
> delivers measurable digital and economic access to Afghan students, women, and
> small businesses in their own language.**

This is a small, specific, *very* fundable number — frame it that way.

---

## 7. Phased plan

1. **Now — small innovation grants + prizes** (UNICEF Innovation Fund, GSMA
   Innovation Fund, Google.org, challenge funds). Goal: validate, first 100–500
   users, pilot metrics.
2. **Then — a larger program grant** (GIZ/FCDO/UNDP/Asia Foundation/AKDN) to
   scale and cover the fixed baseline, justified by pilot data.
3. **Then — revenue + partnerships + diaspora** — as paying users approach
   break-even (~60–100), pair revenue with telecom/HesabPay partnerships and
   diaspora philanthropy so the platform is not grant-dependent forever.
